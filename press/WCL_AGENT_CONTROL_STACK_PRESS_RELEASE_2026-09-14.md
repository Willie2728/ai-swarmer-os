## Wilkerson Collective Labs Advances Agent Control and Resilience Stack for the Autonomous AI Era

### SOVRAIL, AI SWARMER OS and KAMERON AI OS are being developed as complementary layers for authority control, behavioral security, containment and trusted recovery

ELK GROVE, Calif. — September 14, 2026 — Wilkerson Collective Labs (WCL), an AI-first product studio founded by CEO Willie Wilkerson, today announced continued development of a three-product agent control and resilience stack designed to help organizations govern increasingly autonomous AI systems.

The stack combines SOVRAIL, AI SWARMER OS and KAMERON AI OS. SOVRAIL provides a controlled execution boundary for agents and applications using APIs and model providers, including scoped credentials, rate and budget controls, failover and assurance records around intended and observed effects. AI SWARMER OS is a defensive trust and runtime security control plane designed to evaluate agent activity and capabilities, detect defined high-risk behaviors, apply policy, quarantine or contain suspicious activity, and continuously re-evaluate trust as software, permissions, dependencies and behavior change. KAMERON AI OS provides the resilience layer, preserving integrity-protected execution checkpoints and supporting controlled recovery from previously trusted state.

"The question is no longer only whether an AI system can answer correctly. As agents gain tools, credentials and authority, enterprises need infrastructure that can bound what those agents are allowed to do, observe what they actually do, intervene when behavior departs from policy, and recover safely when something goes wrong," said Willie Wilkerson, Founder and CEO of Wilkerson Collective Labs. "Our work is focused on building those control points as a defense-in-depth system."

The development comes as AI laboratories, standards bodies and security researchers intensify work on autonomous-agent containment, blast-radius limitation, secure tool use and agent-security standards. WCL's architecture is designed around deterministic control boundaries in addition to behavioral monitoring: authorization should be explicit, high-risk capabilities should be inspected before trust is granted, external effects should be attributable to approved intent, suspicious activity should be interruptible, and recovery should require renewed trust rather than simply restoring a previous snapshot.

Current working components include signed telemetry, allow/review/block policy decisions, defined detections for prompt injection, credential access, unapproved tools and destinations, unusual outbound activity and other agent-risk signals; capability fingerprinting and revalidation logic; scoped API credentials and spending controls; intent/context/effect/outcome assurance records; integrity-protected recovery capsules; containment plans; and recovery eligibility checks. WCL is continuing to harden the stack for enterprise pilots with additional enforcement adapters, isolated execution, enterprise identity, persistent capability reputation, broader security scanning, multi-agent correlation and independent adversarial testing.

Wilkerson Collective Labs is developing the security stack within a broader studio thesis focused on autonomous AI, resilient infrastructure and AI-native creative and communications products. Other WCL assets in development include VERLORAY, an autonomous business-generation platform; Bridge Talk, an AI communications concept; Valerai AI Music; and Aurelius, an AI art platform.

WCL is seeking conversations with enterprise design partners, AI-safety and cybersecurity researchers, government and standards stakeholders, qualified strategic investors, and counsel supporting intellectual-property and commercialization strategy.
