# AI SWARMER OS — Repository Operating Charter

## Durable terminology

- **Wisdom Guide** is the platform's chief-of-staff super-agent. It understands the company and product, interprets user intent, delegates to specialist Knowledge Guides, combines their work, requests approvals, and reports results.
- **Knowledge Guides** are bounded specialist agents such as Security Engineering, Research, CTO, CFO, Go-to-Market, Compliance, and Customer Success. Each has a narrow mandate, least-privilege tools, and auditable outputs.
- Both guide classes are designed for photorealistic, conversational presentation through a provider such as Tavus. Their authority comes from the AI SWARMER OS policy and tool layer, never from the avatar.
- Do not rename these concepts to “super agent” or “sub-agent” in customer-facing copy unless explaining the architecture.

## Product truth rules

- Never describe an unconfigured provider, simulated workflow, seeded event, or planned connector as live production capability.
- Separate observed evidence, analytical inference, and unverified allegation.
- An IP address, route, fingerprint, or tool signature does not prove a person's identity. Attribution must be probabilistic, sourced, lawfully obtained, and human-reviewed.
- AI SWARMER OS is defensive. Never implement hack-back, unauthorized access, persistence on third-party systems, doxxing, or covert surveillance.
- High-impact actions require exact target, stated reason, authorization, policy evaluation, and audit logging. Prefer reversible containment.
- Protect trade secrets, credentials, customer telemetry, personal information, source code, prompts, and internal financials. Guides may describe capabilities without revealing protected implementation details.

## Product quality bar

- Build working vertical slices with tests and explicit configuration states.
- Use secure-by-default settings, least privilege, signed telemetry, tenant isolation, allowlists, rate limits, tamper-evident audit records, and human approval gates.
- Claims for investors and buyers must be supportable by a demo, test result, contract, or cited source.
- For critical infrastructure, fail safely: monitor before enforcing, preserve deterministic safety controls, and never allow an AI decision to interrupt life-safety operations without a pre-approved runbook.
